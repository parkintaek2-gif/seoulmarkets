"Doctor X: Mafia in White," SBS's remake of the long-running Japanese medical drama, has been Netflix's No. 1 non-English series worldwide for two weeks. In seven places it is No. 1 outright, among them Indonesia and Vietnam.

Netflix's per-country file for the week of 12–18 October shows something else in those two countries. At No. 10, below the remake, sits the Japanese original, "Doctor-X: Surgeon Michiko Daimon," first broadcast by TV Asahi in 2012.

## A first in both countries

Netflix's per-country file runs back to July 2021, 277 weeks across 94 countries. In all of that, the Japanese series had reached a national Top 10 only a handful of times before this month.

| Week | Country | Original's rank |
|---|---|---|
| 23 Oct 2022 | Japan | 9 |
| 6 Sep 2026 | Hong Kong | 10 |
| 13 Sep 2026 | Hong Kong | 8 |
| 18 Oct 2026 | Indonesia | 10 |
| 18 Oct 2026 | Vietnam | 10 |

The two October entries are its first ever in Indonesia and Vietnam. They come in the second week of the Korean version, and in the two countries where that version is No. 1.

The Hong Kong entries in September came before the remake premiered on 9 October, so the original was already finding an audience there on its own. Indonesia and Vietnam are different: nothing in the file puts it in either Top 10 until the week the remake led both.

## The remake's own reach

The Korean series entered national Top 10s in 36 countries in its premiere week and 48 the week after. It was No. 1 in the same seven both weeks, all in Asia: Hong Kong, Indonesia, the Philippines, Singapore, South Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam. It airs on Fridays and Saturdays, so each week adds episodes.

The Japanese original has never appeared in Netflix's global Top 10.

## What this does and does not say

The file records ranks, not people. It cannot say whether the viewers who pushed the original into Indonesia's and Vietnam's Top 10 had just watched the remake, though the timing matches. A No. 10 place also needs far fewer viewers than a No. 1, and the per-country file gives no hours or views to measure the gap.

What it does show is narrow and checkable. In the week a Korean remake led two national charts, the 14-year-old Japanese series it is based on charted in both for the first time.
