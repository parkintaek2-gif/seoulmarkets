Every Korean viewing-hours figure anyone publishes, ours included, starts with a question nobody
asks out loud: how do you know that chart row is the Korean show?

Netflix publishes a title and a country's top ten. It does not publish an identifier. So the match
is made on the name — and names are not unique.

## We checked all 243 of them

We asked Wikidata, for every Korean title we count, which countries have produced a film or TV
series carrying exactly that English label. 975 works came back.

| | Titles | Share of hours |
|---|---|---|
| Name no one else uses | 163 | 83.6% |
| Name shared with at least one foreign work | 76 | 15.9% |
| Could not confirm from the label | 2 | 0.2% |
| Nothing returned | 2 | 0.4% |

One hour in six that we count as Korean sits under a name another country has also used.

## The biggest ones

| Title | Hours | Also made in |
|---|---|---|
| The Glory | 561m | China |
| Bloodhounds | 353m | United States |
| Love Next Door | 340m | Thailand |
| Business Proposal | 279m | Hong Kong |
| True Beauty | 242m | Canada, United States |
| Little Women | 164m | Japan, UK, United States |
| Hellbound | 163m | United States |
| Sweet Home | 123m | Japan, Poland, Spain |

And some names are not shared with one country but with a crowd. **Desire** is carried by works
from sixteen countries. **The Guest** by twelve, including the Soviet Union. **Oasis** by eight,
**Trigger** by seven.

## Does that mean the numbers are wrong?

No — and we want to be exact about why not.

A shared title means the **name alone** cannot settle which work charted. It does not mean the
entry is a mismatch. In practice a Korean show on a Korean chart week, in a market where Korean
titles run 20 to 30 per cent of slots, is overwhelmingly the Korean one. We looked and did not find
a wrong entry.

What the 15.9 per cent measures is how much of our count rests on a name instead of an identifier.
That is a different thing from an error rate, and it is the honest thing to publish, because the
reader cannot see it otherwise.

## Why print your own blind spot

Because the alternative is a number that looks cleaner than it is. Every viewing-hours table you
have read about Korean content was built this way — on names — and almost none of them say so.

We would rather you knew which sixth of our figure is resting on a label, and which titles those
are, than have you find out from someone else.

*Wikidata country-of-origin for every exact English label we count, resolved 2 September 2026,
against Netflix Tudum weekly top-10 country lists. Exact label matches only. Works with no Wikidata
entry are not counted here, which means the true overlap is higher than 15.9 per cent, not lower.*
