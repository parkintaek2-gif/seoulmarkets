Korea's K-Brand Index published its July 2026 ranking of the country's most valuable celebrity
endorsers — actors, a rapper-turned-mogul, a trot singer, a footballer, a comedian, among others —
built from what it says was over a billion online mentions. Actor So Ji-sub placed first. We pulled
English Wikipedia reading for the eight names on that list who fall inside our own datasets, over
roughly the same month. So Ji-sub stayed first. Almost nothing else did.

## The brand index's order

| Rank | Name | Field |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | So Ji-sub | Actor |
| 2 | G-Dragon | Musician (BIGBANG) |
| 3 | Son Heung-min | Footballer |
| 4 | Yoo Jae-suk | TV host |
| 5 | Lim Young-woong | Trot singer |
| 6 | Byeon Woo-seok | Actor |
| 7 | Kim Go-eun | Actress |
| 8 | Lee Dong-wook | Actor |
| 9 | Kim Do-young | Baseball player |
| 10 | Karina | Musician (aespa) |

Son Heung-min and Kim Do-young are athletes. Our Wikipedia reading builds only cover Korean singers
and actors by Wikidata occupation, so neither has a figure here — we are not substituting an
estimate for them.

## Our reading order, for the eight we can measure

| Rank by reading | Name | Avg. daily English Wikipedia views | Brand index rank |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | So Ji-sub | 7,785 | 1 |
| 2 | Byeon Woo-seok | 2,097 | 6 |
| 3 | Kim Go-eun | 2,019 | 7 |
| 4 | Lee Dong-wook | 1,639 | 8 |
| 5 | Karina | 1,021 | 10 |
| 6 | G-Dragon | 794 | 2 |
| 7 | Yoo Jae-suk | 417 | 4 |
| 8 | Lim Young-woong | 97 | 5 |

Only the top spot survives the move from one measure to the other. Below it, the lists run in
close to opposite directions: the three actors the brand index placed lowest (6th, 7th, 8th) are
the three our reading data places highest after So Ji-sub. The two music names the brand index
ranked just behind him (G-Dragon at 2nd, Lim Young-woong at 5th) are our 6th and 8th — dead last.

## The widest gaps

G-Dragon reads at 794 views a day. Lim Young-woong, who placed three spots above him in the brand
index's 5th slot, reads at 97 — an eighth of G-Dragon's figure, and the lowest of the eight names we
measured. Trot, the genre Lim Young-woong is known for, has built comparatively little audience
outside Korea; this is one visible trace of that gap.

At the other end, So Ji-sub's 7,785 daily views are 3.7 times Byeon Woo-seok's 2,097, the
next-highest name in our data — so the one name both measures agree on is also the only one that
pulls away from the rest of the field by either count.

## What this does not tell you

The K-Brand Index does not publish how it weighs "over a billion" mentions into a rank, so we are
reporting an order, not a score, and we have not verified its inputs. It is very likely built
mostly from Korean-language mentions — which would explain why a trot singer with deep domestic
reach and a comedian-TV host (Yoo Jae-suk) can outrank three screen actors there, and do the reverse
in English Wikipedia reading, where overseas audiences who encountered someone through a specific
drama or film tend to concentrate.

We also did not check why any one person's reading moved — a drama premiere, a tour, a casting
announcement and a controversy all look the same in a pageview count, and we did not match any of
these eight against a news timeline. And we left out the two athletes rather than force a number
that isn't there: Son Heung-min, in particular, is likely to read very differently from everyone
else on this list for reasons that have nothing to do with K-content, and we would rather say
nothing than guess.

*Wikimedia pageviews, en.wikipedia, all-access, user traffic only (bots excluded), daily averages
from two 30-day builds — kpop-occupation entertainers measured 8 July-6 August 2026, actors measured
5 July-3 August 2026 — chosen to overlap as closely as our archived builds allow with the brand
index's 1-31 July 2026 survey window. Where a name appeared in both builds, we used the
occupation-matched one (actors for screen actors, the kpop build for musicians and the TV host).
