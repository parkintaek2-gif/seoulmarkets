Every week the Korean entertainment site Star News publishes the results of its STARPOLL fan vote. This week, in the all-category "King of Kings" ranking, four of the top five names are **trot** singers, performers of the older, ballad-based genre that dominates Korean television's singing contests.

English Wikipedia, which we use as a measure of international look-it-up interest, sees almost none of them.

## The vote and the readers, side by side

| King of Kings rank | Star | Genre | Votes | English Wikipedia reads a day (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lim Young-woong | Trot | 389,228 | 81 |
| 2 | Park Seo-jin | Trot | 181,132 | no English article found |
| 3 | Song Ga-in | Trot | 36,917 | 13 |
| 4 | Kim Soo-hyun | Actor | 32,478 | **1,337** |
| 5 | Lee Chan-won | Trot | 29,896 | 24 |

Lim Young-woong collected **twelve times** Kim Soo-hyun's votes. Kim Soo-hyun's English Wikipedia page is read about **sixteen times** as often as Lim's. Park Seo-jin, second in the vote with 181,132, does not have an English Wikipedia article under his name that we could find.

## The same split in the categories

The individual categories follow the same line. The male trot winner, Park Seo-jin, took 45,114 votes, roughly three times the male idol winner, CORTIS's Sunghyun (15,418), and four times the male actor winner, Kim Soo-hyun (10,256). On English Wikipedia the order turns over: the CORTIS group page averages 1,527 reads a day, Kim Soo-hyun 1,337, and trot singer Young Tak, second in the male trot vote, 50.

## Why the two numbers disagree

They count different things. A STARPOLL vote is a fan campaign: app users vote repeatedly, and fandoms organise to push their star up the board. Trot's audience in Korea is widely described as older, and the vote shows how hard that audience pushes. English Wikipedia reads show something else: how many people outside Korea, or reading in English, look a name up.

Trot is one of the biggest genres inside Korea and one of the least exported. These two numbers put that in one table.

## What this does and does not say

Votes are not popularity, and English Wikipedia is not the world; it leaves out Korean-language readers entirely. We do not rank anyone by either number alone. The narrower point is that the stars Korea votes for hardest are, on this measure, the ones English-language readers look up least.
