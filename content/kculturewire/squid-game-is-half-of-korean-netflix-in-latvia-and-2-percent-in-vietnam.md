Netflix publishes a weekly top 10 for 93 countries. Over 268 weeks — five years — we counted how
many of those slots went to Korean titles, and the answer depends enormously on where you are
sitting.

## Two different internets

| Country | Korean share of all top-10 slots | Weeks with a Korean title (of 268) |
|---|---|---|
| South Korea | 53.6% | 268 |
| Vietnam | 30.2% | 268 |
| Indonesia | 28.9% | 268 |
| Japan | 20.4% | 262 |
| India | 10.0% | 255 |
| Brazil | 7.1% | 219 |
| France | 3.6% | 137 |
| United Kingdom | 1.8% | 82 |
| **United States** | **1.6%** | **67** |

In Vietnam and Indonesia, a Korean title was on the chart every single week for five years, without
one exception. In the United States, three weeks in four had none at all, and the Korean share —
1.6 per cent — is second-lowest of the 93 countries Netflix reports. Only Iceland is lower.

The median country sits at 4.6 per cent.

## Where the share is small, one show is nearly all of it

| Country | Biggest Korean title | Its share of that country's Korean slots |
|---|---|---|
| Latvia | Squid Game | 47% |
| Estonia | Squid Game | 43% |
| United States | Squid Game | 31.8% |
| United Kingdom | Squid Game | 24.2% |
| France | Squid Game | 16% |
| India | Squid Game | 12.8% |
| Japan | Crash Landing on You | 6.6% |
| South Korea | I am Solo | 3.9% |
| Vietnam | Reply 1988 | 2.4% |
| Indonesia | Squid Game | 2.3% |

Nearly half of everything Korean that has ever charted in Latvia is one show. In Vietnam, the
biggest Korean title of five years accounts for 2.4 per cent, and it is not Squid Game — it is
Reply 1988, a 2015 tvN drama.

The same split shows in how many titles it takes to cover half a country's Korean slots. Latvia
needs two, out of the 32 Korean titles that ever charted there. Ukraine needs two of 19. Vietnam
needs 52 of 261, and South Korea 124 of 592. The median country needs 20 of 101.

## What the number is, and is not

A slot is a position on a weekly top 10, not an audience. A show watched steadily by a few hundred
thousand people and never in a country's top ten does not appear here at all — and in a market
where the Korean share is 1.6 per cent, that will be hiding a great deal.

We also cannot tell you why any of these shares are what they are. Catalogue rights, subtitle and
dub availability, release timing and marketing all differ country by country, and Netflix publishes
none of it. What the table shows is the outcome, not the cause.
