KBS2's weekend drama ***Love on the Menu*** has been shown on Saturday and Sunday evenings. In **every one of the 14 weekends** in Nielsen Korea's day tables from 1 August to 15 November 2026 that has both nights, the Sunday episode rated higher than the Saturday episode. The Sunday mean is **15.84%**, the Saturday mean **14.35%**. This piece sets the two nights side by side.

## The 14 weekends

National household ratings, Saturday then Sunday, with the Sunday lead in points:

The lead ranges from 0.6 to 2.4 points and averages 1.49. Sunday reached 16.0% or more on 9 of the 14 weekends. Saturday reached 15.0% or more on three: 12 September (15.3%), 24 October (15.2%) and 14 November (15.0%). The series high in this window is **16.7%** on Sunday 18 October, with 2,991,818 viewers nationwide. The lowest is 11.7% on Saturday 1 August.

## Rank on the day

On all 14 Sundays, and on the lone Sunday of 4 October (15.5%), the drama was first among all programmes on the terrestrial top-20 table. On Saturdays the picture changed on 10 October. Through 19 September it was first on all eight Saturdays in the window. On each of the six Saturdays since, it was second. The drama above it was SBS's *Doctor X*, which began on 9 October and ended on 14 November, and whose Saturday rating was higher on each of those dates.

## Seoul area and viewer counts

The Seoul-area rating runs lower than the national one on every one of these days. On Sunday 15 November it was 14.1% in the Seoul area against 15.9% nationwide, with 2,845,495 viewers nationwide and 1,578,967 in the Seoul area. On Saturday 14 November it was 13.4% against 15.0%, with 2,693,542 and 1,502,111.

## Earlier and later weekends

The first four weekends in the window average 13.55% on Saturday and 14.90% on Sunday. The last four average 14.90% on Saturday and 16.20% on Sunday. Both nights are 1.3 to 1.35 points higher than at the start of the window. The window starts on 1 August, so this says nothing about the weeks before.

## What this does not say

This is a count of weekends in a window, not an account of why viewers watch on one night more than the other. We give no reason for the Sunday lead. Two Saturdays are missing from the tables and are excluded above. A rating is a share of households that watched, not a measure of how good the drama is.
