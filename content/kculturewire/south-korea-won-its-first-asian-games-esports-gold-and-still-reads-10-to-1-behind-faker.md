South Korea won its first Asian Games gold medal in esports fighting games on 27 September, and the
team that did it will be the least-read story of the Games week — next to a teammate who, as of
this piece, hasn't played a single match yet.

## The gold: a 41.7-year-old average age, down 1-2 and still winning

Bae "Knee" Jae-min (Tekken 8), Lee Kwang-no (The King of Fighters XV) and Yeon Je-gil (Street
Fighter 6) each played their own game as South Korea's fighting-games team, facing host nation Japan
in the final at Aichi Sky Expo. South Korea trailed 1-2 in set score, then won four sets in a row to
take the match 5-2 — the country's first-ever Asian Games gold in the discipline.

## Two days later, the gold medalist reads a tenth of his still-unplayed teammate

South Korea's League of Legends team, competing separately at these Games, includes Lee "Faker"
Sang-hyeok — arguably the best-known Korean name in esports outside Korea. His group stage doesn't
start until 30 September; he has not played a match at this year's Games.

English Wikipedia readers looked up Knee's page (under either "Knee (gamer)" or the redirect "Bae
Jae-min" — both lead to the same article) 152 times on 27 September, the day of the win, and 116
times the next day. Over those same two days, "Faker (gamer)" was read 1,076 and 1,148 times — seven
to ten times as often, for a player who had not yet stepped onto the stage this Games.

Faker's own Asian Games history shows why the name carries that weight regardless of this year's
result: silver in 2018 at Jakarta-Palembang, a 1-3 final loss to China, and gold in 2022 at Hangzhou.
Whatever happens in his group stage this week, the reading gap was already set before he played a
single game of it.
