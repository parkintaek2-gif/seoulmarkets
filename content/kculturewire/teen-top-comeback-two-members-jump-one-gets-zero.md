Teen Top, the K-pop group that debuted in 2010, released its 11th mini album, "Don't Stop," on October 15 at 6 p.m. KST. Four members were shown in the comeback's concept photo rollout: Niel, Chunji, Ricky and Changjo.

English Wikipedia reading, measured independently of any chart or sales report, moved for some of them and not at all for others.

## Release day, four different outcomes

| Page | Pre-release baseline (median) | Release day (15 Oct) | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teen Top (group) | 116 | 816 | 7.0x |
| Changjo | 23 | 144 | 6.3x |
| Niel | 42 | 171 | 4.1x |
| Ricky | 4 | 0 | 0x |
| Chunji | 0 | 0 | — |

The group's own Wikipedia page saw its reading multiply sevenfold on release day, and two of the four promoting members, Changjo and Niel, saw multi-fold jumps of their own. The other two did not move. Ricky's reading, already the lowest of the four promoting members at a median of 4 reads a day over the prior 25 days, fell to zero on the day his own group's comeback dropped. Chunji's page recorded a reader on only 3 of those 25 baseline days and stayed at zero on release day too.

## The split follows the baseline, not the comeback

Niel and Changjo were already the two most-read of the four promoting members before "Don't Stop" existed as a release date. Whatever this comeback did for reading, it moved the two pages people were already visiting more, and left the two quieter pages exactly where they were.

## What this does and doesn't say

This measures one specific audience — people who looked up an English Wikipedia page the day a 15-year-old group released new music — not streaming numbers, chart placement, or Korean-language interest, none of which we measured here. We also only have one day of post-release data: Wikimedia's own pageview dataset had not published October 16 or 17 at the time of this measurement, so we're reporting a release-day snapshot, not a settled pattern across a full promotion cycle.

*English Wikipedia pageviews, all-access, user traffic only (bots excluded), daily, 20 September-15 October 2026; measured 18 October 2026. Medians, not means, so single days do not carry the whole baseline window.*
