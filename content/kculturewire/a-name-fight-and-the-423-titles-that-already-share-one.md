Demon Hunter, a Christian metal band formed in 2001, has filed a trademark suit against Netflix over
*KPop Demon Hunters*. We have no view on the merits — that is a question for a court and not for a
dataset.

What we can say is what shared names look like in this corner of the business, because we have counted
them.

## 423 of 974

Of the **974 Korean titles** that reached some country's Netflix weekly top ten between July 2021 and
August 2026:

| | | |
|---|---:|---:|
| The name belongs to a Korean work only | 525 | 53.9% |
| **The same English name also belongs to a work from elsewhere** | **423** | **43.4%** |
| Could not be classified | 15 | 1.5% |
| Charted as Korean, but no Korean work of that name found | 11 | 1.1% |

**Two in five Korean titles on these charts share their English name with something else.** Not a
lawsuit, not a copy — just the ordinary result of short English titles and a hundred years of film and
television.

The biggest ones by chart places:

| Title | Also from | Chart places |
|---|---|---:|
| The Glory | China | 601 |
| Bloodhounds | United States | 583 |
| True Beauty | Canada, United States | 489 |
| Business Proposal | Hong Kong | 464 |
| Love Next Door | Thailand | 368 |
| Little Women | Japan, UK, United States | 288 |

The country whose catalogue collides with Korean titles most often is the **United States, on 285
titles**, then the United Kingdom on 152 and France on 103.

## Why this matters for reading a chart

If you look up a Korean title and find a different film, you have met this problem. It is also ours:
when we build a page for a title, we have to decide whether the chart rows in front of us belong to the
Korean work or to its namesake. For 423 titles that decision is not automatic, and
[we publish the verdict on each one rather than hiding it](/is-it-korean).

Eleven titles are worse than shared: they charted as Korean, and Wikidata records no Korean work under
that name at all. We print those as unconfirmed instead of quietly calling them Korean.

## The title at the centre of it

*KPop Demon Hunters* is **not** one of our 974. It is a US-produced animated film about K-pop, and our
list is Korean productions — so it sits outside the count above, and we would rather say that than let
the number stand next to it and imply otherwise.

We do measure it separately, because it charted almost everywhere: **93 countries**, and Latvia held it
in its top ten for **62 weeks**, against 49 in the United States and 32 in South Korea.
[Country by country, week by week, is here](/demon-hunters-countries).

## What we are not saying

We are not saying the suit is strong or weak, and we are not saying that 423 shared names make any
particular claim less serious. A count of coincidences says nothing about one case. What it does say is
that in this data, sharing an English title is the normal condition rather than the exception — which
is a fact worth having before reading a headline about a name.
