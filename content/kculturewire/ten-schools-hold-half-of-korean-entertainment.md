If you have watched Korean film or television for any length of time, you have watched one school.
Cho Jung-seok, Bae Suzy, Park Seo-joon, Son Ye-jin and Lee Joon-gi all passed through Seoul
Institute of the Arts, and so did 347 other people on our roster — 352 in all, more than any other
school by a clear margin.

That is the headline. The structure underneath it is the more interesting part.

## Ten schools, half the field

We hold 9,249 people. Wikidata records a school for 4,535 of them. Among the 1,529 schools that
appear, 55 have at least fifteen of our people — enough that a count means something — and those 55
hold 3,399 people between them.

| | People | Share of the 3,399 |
|---|---|---|
| Top school | 352 | 10.4% |
| Top 3 | 868 | 25.5% |
| Top 5 | 1,213 | 35.7% |
| Top 10 | 1,858 | 54.7% |

Ten schools out of 1,529 account for more than half.

"Most translated" here means the number of Wikipedia language editions that carry a page for that
person — 57 for Cho Jung-seok, 52 for Kim Soo-hyun, 49 for Jun Ji-hyun and Gong Yoo. It is a measure
of how far a name has travelled, not of how good anyone is.

## The second number: whether the name is readable in English at all

Each school also has a share of its people whose names appear on English Wikipedia. It is not the
same everywhere.

| School | Name on English Wikipedia |
|---|---|
| Dongguk University | 93% |
| Chung-Ang University | 91% |
| Kyung Hee University | 90% |
| Seoul Institute of the Arts | 87% |
| Dongduk Women's University | 72% |

Dongduk sits twenty-one points below Dongguk. Both are large, both are in Seoul, and both produced
names you would recognise — Jung Ho-yeon on one side, Jun Ji-hyun on the other. What differs is how
much of the rest of each cohort an English-language reader can look up at all.

We are not going to tell you why. English Wikipedia coverage follows English-language sourcing, and
we did not measure sourcing.

## What we did not build, and why

Half of our roster has no school recorded — 4,714 people. That half is not an error in the data
and it is not a zero; it is simply not written down, and we count it as unknown.

We also did not build the page people search for most in this area. "K-pop idol height" draws more
autocomplete demand than anything about schools, and Wikidata records a height for 320 of our 9,249
people — 3.5 per cent. A "tallest idol" ranking built on 3.5 per cent would be a page that states a
wrong answer with total confidence, so there isn't one.
