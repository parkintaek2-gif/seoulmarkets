Interest cover asks whether a company's operating profit is big enough to pay what it owes in financing. We could not compute true interest cover for Korea's listed companies, because the filings the tape holds carry a broader line, **finance costs**. We used that as a rough screen.

## The result

Of **1,293** companies with a positive finance-cost line and an operating income on file, **627 (48.5%)** reported finance costs larger than operating income.

| Market | Companies tested | Finance costs above operating income | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| KOSPI | 408 | 143 | 35.0% |
| KOSDAQ | 881 | 481 | 54.6% |
| All | 1,293 | 627 | 48.5% |

## Two different groups

The 627 split in two. **446** had an operating loss, so no ratio can be formed. **181** earned an operating profit that was smaller than finance costs; **92** of those still reported a positive net income for the year.

At the other end, **424** companies (32.8%) earned at least three times their finance costs.

## Read with care

Finance costs include more than interest, and we did not net cash or interest income. A company on this list is not shown to be in difficulty. The table counts a ratio, nothing more.

*This is statistics, not you or your company.*
