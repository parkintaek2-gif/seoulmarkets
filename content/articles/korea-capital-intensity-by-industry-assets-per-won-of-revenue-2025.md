Capital intensity is how much capital a business needs to make a won of sales. We measure it simply: total assets divided by annual revenue, from each company's fiscal-2025 annual report. A figure of 2.0 means two won of assets for each won of sales.

## The middle company

Across **2,469** listed companies with both figures, the median was **1.63**.

## By industry

There are 28 industries with at least 20 companies measured. These are the five highest and the five lowest medians.

| Industry | Companies | Median assets per won of revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Real Estate | 27 | 33.30 |
| Research and Development | 62 | 10.12 |
| Financial and Insurance Related Services | 20 | 4.40 |
| Medicinal Substances and Pharmaceuticals | 179 | 2.19 |
| Publishing | 184 | 2.14 |
| Primary Metals | 85 | 1.13 |
| Architectural and Engineering Services | 21 | 1.04 |
| Computer Programming and Systems Integration | 37 | 1.02 |
| Motor Vehicles and Trailers | 105 | 0.97 |
| Food Products Manufacturing | 71 | 0.93 |

The top median is 36 times the bottom one: 33.30 against 0.93.

## Two readings of a high figure

A high ratio can mean a lot of capital in a business that sells slowly, as in property. It can also mean a company that holds a lot of cash or other assets but has little revenue. The ratio itself does not tell these apart.

## What this does not say

This is a balance-sheet measure of capital, not market value. We left market capitalisation out on purpose: it is the price of expectations, not capital employed. We also do not say which level is better. Financial companies may be measured on a different basis, so the Financial Services median of **2.01** across 89 companies is shown for completeness only.
