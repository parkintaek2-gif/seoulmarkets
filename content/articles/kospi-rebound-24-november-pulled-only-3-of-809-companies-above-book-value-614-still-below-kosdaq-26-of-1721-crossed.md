The KOSPI rose 6.32% on 24 November, yet the share of KOSPI companies trading below the book value on their 2025 balance sheets barely moved. Of the 809 KOSPI companies with positive equity and a market value, **614 (75.9%)** closed below book on the 24th. A day earlier it was 617. Only **3** companies moved from below 1.0 to above it.

## Four dates, one measure

| KOSPI, 809 companies | 12 Nov | 20 Nov | 23 Nov | 24 Nov |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Below book value | 606 | 609 | 617 | 614 |
| Share of companies | 74.9% | 75.3% | 76.3% | 75.9% |
| Share of market value | 23.1% | 24.3% | 24.6% | 24.2% |
| Median price-to-book | 0.498 | 0.473 | 0.464 | 0.464 |

Between 12 and 24 November the index fell about 6%, and the below-book count rose by eight companies. The median ratio fell from 0.498 to 0.464. Three of every four KOSPI companies were below book before the drop and are still so after the rebound, and the companies below book hold about a quarter of the market's value, because the companies above the line hold the other 75.8%.

## Why so few crossed

Few companies sit close to the line. On 23 November only 16 KOSPI companies had a ratio between 0.9 and 1.0, and 3 of them crossed on the rebound. Between 0.8 and 1.0 there were 48 companies, and still only 3 crossed. The rest of the 614 are further away: the median KOSPI company is at 0.46, which would need a gain of about 116% to reach 1.0.

## The KOSDAQ is closer to the line

| KOSDAQ, 1,721 companies | 12 Nov | 23 Nov | 24 Nov |
|---|---|---|---|
| Below book value | 981 | 1,035 | 1,011 |
| Share of companies | 57.0% | 60.1% | 58.7% |
| Median price-to-book | 0.848 | 0.779 | 0.797 |

On the KOSDAQ, 62 companies were between 0.9 and 1.0 on the 23rd and 24 of them crossed. In all 26 moved above 1.0 and 2 moved below. The KOSDAQ median ratio, 0.80, is much nearer 1.0 than the KOSPI's 0.46, so the same percentage rise changes more of its counts.

## What this does not show

Book value is the equity in the 2025 annual report. Companies have changed since, and the ratio compares a November price with a December balance sheet. The count says how many companies sit under an accounting line, not whether any of them is cheap. It is not advice to buy or sell any share.
