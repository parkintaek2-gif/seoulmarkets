Between **25 August and 11 November 2026**, listed Korean companies filed **2,642** material disclosures that this desk kept after selecting events that move a decision, in the way a US Form 8-K does. **201** of them, or **7.6%**, concern delisting risk: a delisting review, a watch-list designation, a trading halt or a market-value shortfall. The Financial Supervisory Service's DART system is the source.

## Where the filings fall

| Market | All kept filings | Delisting-risk filings | Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| KOSDAQ | 1,678 | 170 | 10.1% |
| KOSPI | 905 | 26 | 2.9% |
| KONEX | 35 | 5 | 14.3% |
| Other | 24 | 0 | 0% |
| **All** | **2,642** | **201** | **7.6%** |

KOSDAQ filers made **170 of the 201** delisting-risk filings, or **84.6%**. They made 63.5% of all kept filings. The KOSPI made 26, or 12.9% of the delisting-risk filings, from 34.3% of all kept filings.

## What else is in the file

The largest event type among the 2,642 kept filings is a single supply contract, with **701** (26.5%). Material reports follow with **465** (17.6%), then paid-in capital increases with **326** (12.3%). Delisting risk is fourth, with 201, ahead of convertible bond issues (128), material management matters (127) and stake acquisitions (122).

## What the count does not say

These are counts of filings, not of companies, so one company filing three times counts three times. The file records the warning and not what came after it, so it does not show how many of these companies were delisted. KOSDAQ lists far more small companies than the KOSPI, so the higher KOSDAQ share shows where the warnings fall and not that each KOSDAQ company is riskier. The window ends on 11 November, so anything filed since is not here.
