Korea's trade table is corrected in place, and when a figure changes the old one is gone. We keep a full copy of it every few days. The copies of 17 and 25 November differ in **802 figures**, all stamped with a last-changed date of 16 November. That is the third monthly round in our copies, after 656 changed figures stamped 16 September and 729 stamped 16 October.

## Nine months touched

| Month (2026) | Figures changed |
|---|---|
| January | 59 |
| February | 52 |
| March | 65 |
| April | 66 |
| May | 68 |
| June | 85 |
| July | 104 |
| August | 129 |
| September | 174 |

As in October, the more recent the month, the more figures changed: September, the newest month before the round, had 174, nearly three times January's 59. Of the 802, 440 went up and 362 went down.

## Small in total, larger underneath

The national January-September export total moved by +$69.0 million, 0.008%, and imports by +$156.9 million, so the nine-month trade balance fell $88.0 million. The monthly totals moved more than the sum. September exports rose $204.2 million (+0.17%) and August exports fell $124.9 million (-0.13%). Only 18 of the 802 changed figures are national totals. The rest are single countries, where a few are large: Pakistan's August exports were cut from $180.3 million to $88.8 million (-50.7%) and Chile's September exports raised from $148.6 million to $203.9 million (+37.2%).

## January, four times

January is the month with the longest record in our copies. Its trade balance was $8,700.5 million in the copy of 18 September, $8,689.3 million on 21 September, $8,607.1 million on 23 October and $8,583.9 million on 25 November. That is a fall of $116.6 million, all of it from imports, which rose in each round from $57,143 million to $57,259 million. January exports barely moved over the same four copies, from $65,844 million to $65,843 million.

## Three rows that left

Three rows for the International Monetary Fund as an export partner, for January, February and March, are in the 17 November copy and not in the 25 November one. They held $69 thousand, $1.9 million and $2.4 million. We do not know whether they were deleted or merged into another row.

## What this does not show

The table does not give reasons, and we cannot tell whether the earlier or the later figure is closer to the truth. A revision is not evidence of an error in either direction. It is a reminder that a figure read today may read differently next month. This is a count of changes, not a judgment about the data and not advice to buy or sell any share.
