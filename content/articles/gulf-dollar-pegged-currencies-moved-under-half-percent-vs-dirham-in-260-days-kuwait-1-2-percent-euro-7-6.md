In the UAE central bank's table the dirham is the base. The dollar is quoted at **3.6725** dirhams on every one of the 260 business days from 3 November 2025 to 30 October 2026, and on every one of the 2,305 days since January 2018. It never changes.

Several other Gulf currencies are widely described as tied to the dollar too. In the same table they are not constant against the dirham. They move a little.

## How much, in 260 days

Rates are dirhams per unit, so the yen and won rows are small numbers.

The Saudi riyal, which changed on 206 of 259 days, never moved more than 0.07% in a day and ended the window 0.07% below where it began. The Jordanian dinar was almost fixed: two changes in a year, on 11 and 12 November 2025, when it moved up and then back to its old rate. The Qatari riyal and Bahraini dinar changed on about half the days but with larger single steps of 0.34% and 0.42%, and ranged 0.36% and 0.45%.

The floating currencies are an order of magnitude apart. The euro ranged 7.57%, the yen 7.24% and the won 17.22%, and each changed on at least 251 of the 259 days. The pegged group is not frozen, but it is small.

## Kuwait and Hong Kong sit between the two

Kuwait's dinar moved 1.18% against the dirham, six times the Saudi riyal's range and more than twice that of the Bahraini dinar. Its largest one-day move, 0.60% on 7 January, is bigger than any of the Saudi, Qatari, Bahraini or Omani moves. We did not test how Kuwait sets its rate.

The Hong Kong dollar, also described as tied to the dollar, ranged 1.00% and changed on 254 of 259 days.

## The latest month

In the 22 business days of October 2026 the Saudi riyal's range was 0.13%, the Qatari riyal's 0.09%, the Bahraini dinar's 0.17% and the Omani rial's 0.01%, which changed on only four days. The Jordanian dinar did not change at all. In the same month the Kuwaiti dinar ranged 0.13%, the Hong Kong dollar 0.12%, the euro 1.59%, the yen 1.34% and the won 2.71%.

## What this does not show

The table does not say why these rates move, and we do not know whether the Saudi riyal's 206 changes reflect the market or the way cross rates are computed. We did not verify the pegs themselves. Eleven currencies are shown here; the file holds 79, some with unit changes or implausible jumps, which we excluded. The window ends on 30 October. Nothing here is advice on currencies.
