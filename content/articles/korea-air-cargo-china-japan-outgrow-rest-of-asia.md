Korea Airports Corporation's own monthly cargo tonnage figures show air freight arriving in Korea growing far faster from two specific sources — China and Japan — than from the rest of the world combined, even as the overall total barely accelerated.

## Two sources growing three times faster than the total

Comparing the same month, July, across three years removes the seasonal swings a raw month-to-month figure would carry.

| Arriving cargo (tonnes) | Jul 2024 | Jul 2025 | Jul 2026 | Change (2yr) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| China | 36,787 | 39,894 | 45,557 | +23.8% |
| Japan | 20,506 | 20,777 | 26,308 | +28.3% |
| All regions (total) | 176,454 | — | 194,155 | +10.0% |

China and Japan combined now account for 37.0% of all air cargo arriving in Korea, up from 32.4% two years earlier. Every other region's freight, taken together, has been growing at roughly a third of that pace.

## The "rest of Asia" bucket is the largest source and the slowest-growing

KOSIS's regional breakdown keeps "Asia" (excluding Japan and China) as its own catch-all category — and it remains the single largest source of arriving air cargo, larger than China or Japan individually. But it is also the slowest-growing of the major sources:

| Region | Jul 2024 | Jul 2026 | Change (2yr) | Share, Jul 2024 → Jul 2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Rest of Asia | 56,150t | 57,397t | +2.2% | 31.8% → 29.6% |
| China | 36,787t | 45,557t | +23.8% | 20.8% → 23.5% |
| Japan | 20,506t | 26,308t | +28.3% | 11.6% → 13.5% |

A region can stay the largest supplier by volume while losing share, if the volume itself is nearly flat and its competitors are not.

## Not fuller planes — more of them

The passenger side of this same dataset showed China's passenger count per flight rising as traffic grew, suggesting bigger aircraft or higher load factors on that route. Cargo tells a different story: tonnage per flight barely moved for either China (7.36 → 7.51 tonnes) or Japan (3.53 → 3.59) over the same two years. The growth in freight volume tracks the growth in flight count almost one-for-one — this looks like more flights carrying about the same load each, not the same flights carrying more.

## One region moved the same way on both counts

Oceania's arriving cargo fell 25.4% (2,114 → 1,578 tonnes) from July 2024 to July 2026 — in the same direction as the passenger decline this outlet found in a separate pass through this table's passenger columns. Both figures come from the same monthly KOSIS release; this piece did not go looking for a reason behind either move.

This is a record of Korea Airports Corporation's own monthly cargo counts, not a trade-volume forecast.
