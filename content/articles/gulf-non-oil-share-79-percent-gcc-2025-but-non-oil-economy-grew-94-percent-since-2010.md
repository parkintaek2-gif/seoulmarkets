The Gulf Cooperation Council's statistics office puts the non-oil part of the six Gulf economies at **79.0%** of their combined GDP in 2025. That is up from 59.2% in 2010. On the same office's real, constant-price series the non-oil economy stands at **194.2** with 2010 as 100, while the oil economy stands at **117.5**. The share and the real index are two different measures, and they do not move together year by year.

## The share jumped when the real oil index did not fall

The all-six share, with GDP at current prices in US dollars:

| Year | Non-oil share of GDP | Real non-oil index | Real oil index | Total GDP, US$ billion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 59.2% | 100.0 | 100.0 | 1,168 |
| 2011 | 52.9% | 107.8 | 113.1 | 1,479 |
| 2014 | 59.7% | 129.7 | 118.1 | 1,709 |
| 2015 | 74.5% | 136.4 | 121.8 | 1,463 |
| 2022 | 66.8% | 166.0 | 124.7 | 2,326 |
| 2025 | 79.0% | 194.2 | 117.5 | 2,419 |

Between 2014 and 2015 the share rose **14.8 percentage points**. In the same year the real non-oil index rose 5.2% and the real oil index rose 3.1%. Total GDP in dollars fell 14.4%. In 2022 the share fell 6.5 points from 2021 (73.3% to 66.8%) while the real oil index rose from 112.1 to 124.7. So in these two years the share moved in a direction that the real indices do not explain. Its denominator is a dollar value at current prices, and we did not split it into price and volume.

## Two rankings of six states

For 2025, the same file ranks the six states differently depending on the measure.

| State | Non-oil share, 2025 | Share in first year | Real non-oil index | Real oil index |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bahrain | 87.2% | 79.2% (2010) | 169.0 | 106.3 |
| Saudi Arabia | 83.2% | 59.0% (2010) | 203.9 | 118.1 |
| United Arab Emirates | 81.7% | 71.0% (2010) | 184.6 | 138.0 |
| Oman | 66.4% | 58.4% (2010) | 166.9 | 120.9 |
| Qatar | 65.1% | 41.2% (2011) | 183.7 | 94.5 |
| Kuwait | 59.8% | 44.1% (2010) | 141.4 | 100.4 |

By share, Qatar is fifth. By the real non-oil index it is third, ahead of Bahrain, which has the highest share. Saudi Arabia has the largest real non-oil index, 203.9, and the share gain of 24.2 points from 2010 is the largest of the five states with a 2010 start. Qatar's gain from 2011 is 23.9 points. Qatar is also the only one of the six whose real oil index is below its starting level, at 94.5, so its share rose while its real oil economy shrank. Kuwait's real oil index is 100.4, almost unchanged.

The indices are not comparable across rows as levels. Saudi Arabia's constant-price series is chain-linked to 2023, Qatar's and Oman's use 2018, and the others use 2010. Each index is set to 100 in its own first year, so 203.9 against 141.4 says only that Saudi Arabia's real non-oil economy has grown more than Kuwait's against each one's own past.

## What this does not say

This is a read of one statistics office's file, not a view on whether any Gulf state has diversified, and not a forecast. The share is a ratio at current prices, so it can move without any change in what the economy produces. The real indices leave out price effects, but they do not say where the growth came from. We did not look at company data, stock markets or government plans here. Nothing in this piece is a reason to buy or sell any asset, and the figures are about aggregates, not about any one household or investor.
