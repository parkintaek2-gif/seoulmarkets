Japanese listed companies are extremely uneven in size. Take the 3,405 companies whose most
recent filing carries a revenue line, add up what they report, and the total is ¥548.4 trillion.
The 122 largest companies by revenue account for half of it. The other 3,283 account for the
other half.

That is the plain count. The rest of this piece is what it looks like when you cut the same
filings by size band, and what we cannot say from them.

## Five bands, one total

| Reported revenue | Companies | Share of companies | Share of total revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Under ¥1 billion | 84 | 2.5% | 0.01% |
| ¥1 billion to under ¥10 billion | 916 | 26.9% | 0.84% |
| ¥10 billion to under ¥100 billion | 1,571 | 46.1% | 10.56% |
| ¥100 billion to under ¥1 trillion | 732 | 21.5% | 41.96% |
| ¥1 trillion and above | 102 | 3.0% | 46.64% |

Read the first and last rows against each other. Three companies in a hundred, the 102 above ¥1
trillion, report 46.6% of all revenue. The 1,000 smallest companies, those under ¥10
billion, are 29.4% of the companies and 0.85% of the revenue.

The middle of the market is the 10 to 100 billion band: 1,571 companies, the largest group, and
only about a tenth of the total. The median company is smaller than that band's upper edge. It
reports ¥27.8 billion.

## How fast the total builds up

Ranked from the largest down, the cumulative share builds quickly.

| Top N by revenue | Share of total |
|---|---:|
| 10 | 14.9% |
| 50 | 34.3% |
| 100 | 46.3% |
| 122 | 50.1% |
| 300 | 69.3% |

The largest single revenue line on the tape is Toyota Motor at ¥18.3 trillion, followed by
Nissan (¥12.0 trillion), Aeon (¥9.4 trillion), Seven & i Holdings (¥8.9 trillion) and Idemitsu
Kosan (¥8.1 trillion). Those five alone are 10.3% of the whole.

One caution sits right there. Toyota's row is a standalone filing, not a consolidated one. On the
tape, 680 of the 3,405 filings are standalone and 2,725 are consolidated, and the two kinds are
not the same size of thing. A standalone filing leaves out the subsidiaries, which for a large
group can be most of its business. Of the 102 companies above ¥1 trillion, 25 are standalone
filings. We kept each company's most recent filing as filed and did not swap one basis for the
other.

## Which industries carry the total

Within the 3,405, the revenue totals by industry are also lopsided. Transportation equipment
(80 companies) is 12.5% of the total, wholesale trade (287 companies) 12.0%, retail trade (310)
11.9%, electric appliances (218) 8.2% and construction (139) 7.7%. Those five industries
together hold 52.3% of the revenue.

The medians tell a different story from the totals. Among industries with at least 30
companies that report revenue, the highest median belongs to nonferrous metals at ¥167.1
billion, then transportation equipment at ¥115.3 billion, wholesale trade at ¥63.2 billion and
construction at ¥61.7 billion. The lowest are information and communication at ¥7.0 billion
(566 companies), services at ¥11.1 billion (512) and pharmaceuticals at ¥14.1 billion (60).

Information and communication is the biggest group by company count, and one of the smallest by
the typical company. That is a statement about filed revenue and not about profit or value, and
we make no claim about either here.

## Why this is not a ranking

The tape counts what each company filed, and three things would make it wrong as a league
table.

Groups appear more than once. A listed parent that consolidates a listed subsidiary reports
revenue that includes the subsidiary's, and the subsidiary reports its own. The total therefore
counts some revenue twice. We did not try to net it out.

The periods differ. Fiscal year-ends on the tape run from 30 June 2025 to 31 August 2026, and
59.9% of companies close in March. Adding them together adds twelve-month windows that overlap
without matching.

And 303 companies are absent because they have no revenue line. 83 of them are banks, 25
securities firms. Their accounts open with a different top line, such as ordinary income, and
we did not turn one kind of figure into another.

## What the shape does and does not tell you

A median of ¥27.8 billion against a total of ¥548.4 trillion over 3,405 companies means the
average is dragged far above the middle. The average company on the tape reports about ¥161
billion, nearly six times the median. For this reason the median is the better description of an
ordinary company, and the total is a description of a few very large ones.

We have not tested why the distribution looks like this, and we would not call it good or bad.
It is how filed revenue is spread across the companies that report it.

Every number here can be pulled again from the same filings. Toyota's own row, as an example, is
at `https://seoulmarkets.com/v1/japan-financials?code=7203`, and each Japanese company has a
page under [/japan/companies](/japan/companies).
