Small listed companies lose money more often than large ones. How much more is the question. We sorted 2,554 Korean listed companies by market value on 13 November 2026 and counted how many reported a net loss for fiscal 2025.

## Six bands

| Market value | Companies | With a net loss | Loss share | Value of the band, trillion won |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under 30 billion won | 477 | 291 | 61% | 9 |
| 30 to 100 billion | 879 | 381 | 43% | 51 |
| 100 to 300 billion | 607 | 180 | 30% | 106 |
| 300 billion to 1 trillion | 314 | 77 | 25% | 163 |
| 1 to 10 trillion | 221 | 42 | 19% | 659 |
| 10 trillion and above | 56 | 3 | 5% | 3,410 |

Loss share falls at each step: 61%, 43%, 30%, 25%, 19%, 5%. Across the list, 974 of 2,554 companies, 38%, reported a net loss.

## Many companies, little value

The two smallest bands hold 1,356 companies, 53% of the list, and are worth 60 trillion won together. The 56 companies at 10 trillion or more are worth 3,410 trillion. The group where losses are most common is also the group that accounts for the least of the market's value.

## Two readings the table cannot separate

A low market value can come from losing money: investors mark the price down after a loss. A loss can also come from being small: fixed costs weigh more on a company with little revenue. The table shows that the two go together. It cannot say which one drives the other, and it does not say anything about any one company.

## What this does not say

Market value is one day and net income is one fiscal year. 155 companies lack a net income figure or a market value and are not counted. A company can fall into a lower band after a loss and into a higher one after a gain, so band membership moves. A loss rate describes a group. This is a statistic about a list and is not investment advice.
