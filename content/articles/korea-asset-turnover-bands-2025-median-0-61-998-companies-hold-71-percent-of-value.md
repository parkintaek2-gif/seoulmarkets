How much a company sells each year for every won it holds in assets is a plain measure called asset turnover. We computed it for 2,469 Korean listed companies from their fiscal 2025 annual reports, dividing revenue by total assets. The median is 0.61. A company at the median sells 0.61 won of goods or services in a year for each won of assets on its books.

## Six bands

| Revenue / total assets | Companies | Share of the group's market value | Median return on equity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 0.1 | 128 | 4.0% | -16.3% |
| 0.1 to 0.25 | 235 | 3.0% | -3.9% |
| 0.25 to 0.5 | 585 | 14.4% | 0.9% |
| 0.5 to 1 | 998 | 71.1% | 3.9% |
| 1 to 2 | 479 | 7.3% | 4.6% |
| 2 and above | 44 | 0.3% | 7.7% |

The middle band is the biggest by both measures. 998 companies, 40% of the 2,469, sit between 0.5 and 1, and they hold 71.1% of the market value, 2,962 trillion won. The companies at the very top and the very bottom of the table are small in value.

## The bottom of the table is unprofitable

The 128 companies selling less than 0.1 won per won of assets have a median return on equity of -16.3%. In the next band up, 235 companies at 0.1 to 0.25, it is -3.9%. From 0.25 upward the median is positive and rises in each band, to 7.7% for the 44 companies at 2 or more.

That is a pattern in the medians of groups of different sizes, so it is worth saying what it is not. It does not show that turning assets faster causes a higher return. Companies that sell very little against their assets include those still building something, companies holding assets that do not yet earn, and companies that have shrunk. The table reports how the groups look, nothing more.

## The value sits in the middle, not the top

The 44 companies that sell two won or more for each won of assets are worth 11 trillion won together, 0.3% of the market value of the group. Fast turnover in this list belongs to small companies. The large share of value in the 0.5 to 1 band reflects that most of the big Korean companies are in it.

## What this does not say

Financial companies measure revenue differently, and we left them in the same bands, so the top bands in particular may hold some of them. One year of revenue against a year-end balance sheet can mislead for a company that changed size during the year. 240 of the 2,709 companies are not in the table because the figures were missing. This is a statistic about a list, not about any one company, and it is not investment advice.
