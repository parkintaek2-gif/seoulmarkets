In our copy of the Bank of Korea data, which ends on 2 October 2026, the base rate was **3.00%**. Consumer prices in September 2026 were 2.88% higher than a year earlier. The gap between the two, the base rate less year-on-year CPI inflation, was **plus 0.12 percentage points**.

## Five months below zero

Over the 33 months from January 2024 to September 2026, the gap was positive in 28 and negative in 5. All five negative months were consecutive, April to August 2026.

| Month | Base rate (month-end) | CPI, year on year | Gap (points) |
|---|---|---|---|
| October 2024 | 3.25% | 1.25% | +2.00 |
| October 2025 | 2.50% | 2.38% | +0.12 |
| November 2025 | 2.50% | 2.45% | +0.05 |
| March 2026 | 2.50% | 2.16% | +0.34 |
| April 2026 | 2.50% | 2.57% | -0.07 |
| May 2026 | 2.50% | 3.14% | -0.64 |
| June 2026 | 2.50% | 3.16% | -0.66 |
| July 2026 | 2.75% | 2.79% | -0.04 |
| August 2026 | 3.00% | 3.09% | -0.09 |
| September 2026 | 3.00% | 2.88% | +0.12 |

October 2024 was the widest positive gap in the period at 2.00 points. June 2026 was the widest negative, and its 3.16% inflation was the highest in the series since December 2023. The average gap over all 33 months was +0.56 points, and over the nine months of 2026 it was about zero.

## The rate path

The base rate fell from 3.50% to 2.50% in four cuts between October 2024 and May 2025, and stayed at 2.50% for 413 days. It then rose to 2.75% on 16 July 2026 and to 3.00% on 27 August 2026. By 2 October 2026 it had been at 3.00% for 36 days. Of the 1,006 days in the series, 284 were at 3.50%, 48 at 3.25%, 126 at 3.00%, 135 at 2.75% and 413 at 2.50%.

The result does not depend on the date chosen within the month. Using the rate on the 15th instead of the last day, the same five months, April to August 2026, are negative.

## Growth alongside it

Real GDP grew 1.8% quarter on quarter in 2026Q1 and 0.6% in 2026Q2, which compounds to about 2.4% for the half year. Over the four quarters from 2025Q3 to 2026Q2 the figure is about 3.7%, including a 0.1% contraction in 2025Q4. Six of the 26 quarters in the series since 2020 were contractions.

## What this does not show

The gap is a simple subtraction of a published inflation rate from a policy rate. It says nothing about the rate people pay on loans or earn on deposits, and nothing about what inflation will be. Our data stop on 2 October 2026, and any decision since then is not in them. Nothing here is a view on where rates should go or advice to buy or sell any security.
