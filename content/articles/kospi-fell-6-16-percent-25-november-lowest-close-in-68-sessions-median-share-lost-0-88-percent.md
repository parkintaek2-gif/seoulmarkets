On Wednesday 25 November the KOSPI closed at **4,697.51**, down **6.16%**. It is the lowest close in the 68 sessions of our index archive, which starts on 18 August, and 33.7% below the window's high of 7,080.92 on 23 September. The index fell 308.52 points, more than the 297.49 points it gained on 24 November, so two sessions after 23 November it stood 0.23% lower.

## A large fall, a small median

The index is weighted by market value, so it can fall much more than the typical share. On 25 November it did.

| 25 November | KOSPI | KOSDAQ |
|---|---|---|
| Index change | -6.16% | -2.29% |
| Traded listings | 909 | 1,750 |
| Rose | 295 | 469 |
| Fell | 594 | 1,204 |
| Unchanged | 20 | 77 |
| Median listing change | -0.88% | -1.32% |
| Listings down 10% or more | 15 | 61 |
| Listings up 10% or more | 12 | 25 |

Of the KOSPI's 909 traded listings, 32.5% rose. The median listing lost 0.88%, about one seventh of the index's fall. On the KOSDAQ the median listing, at -1.32%, fell more than on the KOSPI, but the index fell far less.

## Compared with 23 November

Because the market rose on 24 November, most shares ended 25 November above where they were two sessions earlier. Among 906 KOSPI listings that traded on both days, 647 (71.4%) closed above their 23 November price and 246 below. On the KOSDAQ, 1,048 of 1,748 (60.0%) were higher. The index, at 0.23% below, did not show that.

Among the six with breadth, 25 November has the second-highest number of rising listings, after 5 November, and a KOSDAQ fall smaller than on any of the four sessions from 15 October to 20 November. It is the second-largest KOSPI fall of the seven in percentage terms behind 15 October, when 113 listings rose.

## How to read it

A 6% index fall with a median share down under 1% means the weight was in a few large listings, not that the whole market moved together. That is a statement about this day's arithmetic. Sister articles today look at which listings carried the fall, which indices rose, and how little trading value it took.

## What this does not show

The records give closes, percentage changes and no-trade flags only. They say nothing about intraday swings or why prices moved, and our copy ends with the 25 November close, so nothing here describes 26 November or later. The window has 68 sessions, so "lowest close" means lowest in that window, not in the market's history. This is a count of one day, not a forecast and not advice to buy or sell any share.
