Debt has a clock on it. We took the 742 Korean listed companies that reported both a short-term and a long-term borrowing balance for fiscal 2025 and asked how much of each company's debt comes due inside twelve months.

The median company: **71%**.

## The money and the companies disagree

Across all 742 companies the balances add to 203 trillion won short-term and 116 trillion long — a 64% short-term share of the money. The median company sits at 71%. The gap is the large borrowers: they hold more of the won and term more of it out, so weighting by company raises the number and weighting by won lowers it. Both are reported above; neither is the "real" one.

## Smaller companies borrow shorter

| Market value | Companies | Median short-term share | Above 80% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 30 billion won | 110 | 72% | 42 |
| 30 to 100 billion | 245 | 76% | 109 |
| 100 to 300 billion | 189 | 70% | 75 |
| 300 billion to 1 trillion | 103 | 68% | 39 |
| 1 to 10 trillion | 71 | 58% | 22 |
| 10 trillion and above | 19 | 50% | 5 |

The largest borrowers split their debt roughly in half. The 30-to-100-billion band — the largest group in the measured set at 245 companies — funds three quarters of its debt inside the year, and 109 of those 245 fund more than four fifths that way.

Sitting at the ends: 76 companies report short-term borrowings and no long-term balance at all; 34 report the reverse.

## What the ratio does not say

A high short-term share is a description of a balance sheet on one date, not a prediction. Rolling working capital facilities sit in the short-term line by design and are rolled as a matter of routine; so does a long-term loan in its final year, for some filers but not others. We cannot separate the two from the filing, which is why the 71% should be read as the looser number rather than a measure of distress.

Nor is this a count of the whole market. **1,952 of 2,709 companies file no borrowing lines we can read**, and we do not treat that silence as zero debt — a company with none and a company that reports it elsewhere are indistinguishable here. Those companies are not measured, not zero. What remains is 742 companies that do borrow and do say how.
