South Korea's market stabilisation fund has not been deployed since 2008, according to the Korea Times, and officials have said as recently as 13 November that it should be used with care. The same week the KOSPI fell 7.95%, closing the week to 20 November at 4,869.96.

## What officials said

On Friday 13 November, at a capital-market policy meeting at the Korea Exchange, the head of the Financial Services Commission said the fund can be activated, Sateconomy reported. The fund works by capital call: money is paid in only when it is needed. He said the decision should rest on systemic risk, not on market volatility alone. The report says the president said the fund's effective operation and appropriate size need to be reviewed. It does not quote either of them directly.

The president had been more cautious on 19 October. The Korea Times reports he told reporters that direct market intervention is undesirable.

## What the fund is, and how often it has been used

The Korea Times counts five set-ups of such funds: in 1990, 2003, 2008, 2020 and 2022. In 2020, during the COVID-19 crash, 10.76 trillion won was raised. The paper says Korea has not deployed the funds since 2008. It contrasts that with Taiwan, which has tapped its fund 10 times since 2000, according to an analyst it quotes.

Neither report gives the fund's size today.

## The market it would be for

The KOSPI closed on 13 November at 5,290.53 and on 20 November at 4,869.96, a loss of 420.57 points, or 7.95%, in five sessions. The 20 November fall alone was 5.21%. This desk's weekly piece on 21 November put it fourth-worst of the 26 weeks since late May.

"Stabilisation fund preparing to activate" has been a trending search phrase in Korea since 22 November. We did not find a report stating that preparations have started, so this piece does not say so. We will update it if an official statement or a report with a named source appears.
