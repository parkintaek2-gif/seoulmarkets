The won finished the week of 16 to 20 November 2026 at **1,334.79** per dollar on Yahoo's Friday quote, against **1,333.78** a week earlier. That is a move of **0.08%**, and the won ended slightly weaker. In the same week the KOSPI fell **7.95%**, from 5,290.53 to 4,869.96. This piece puts the two side by side over the 26 weeks since late May.

## The smallest move of 26 weeks

The won's weekly change over the 26 weeks ending 29 May to 20 November ran from a **3.63% strengthening** in the week to 31 July (to 1,420.60) to a **2.53% weakening** in the week to 5 June (to 1,533.07). This week's 0.08% is the smallest absolute move of the 26. The KOSPI's 7.95% fall that week is the fourth-worst of the same 26 weeks.

On the Seoul interbank market the won closed on Friday 20 November at **1,336.20**, up 6.20 won on the day, per Newsis, Money Today and Asia Economy. Yahoo's quote at its own closing time was 1,334.79.

The KOSPI fell by 5% or more in eight of the 26 weeks. Dollar-won change in the same week, positive meaning a weaker won:

The won weakened in four of the eight weeks and strengthened in four. The largest weakening in any of them was 1.37%, in the week to 6 November, when the KOSPI fell 11.71%. In the week of the KOSPI's largest fall in the window, 14.46% to 16 October, the won moved 0.15% stronger. The weeks to 16 July and 8 October had four and three sessions, not five.

## Since 21 May

The won stood at 1,499.77 per dollar on 21 May and at 1,334.79 on 20 November, which is 11.0% stronger over the window. The KOSPI closed at 7,208.95 on 20 May and at 4,869.96 on 20 November, a fall of 32.4%.

## What this does not say

This is a count of weeks in a 26-week window, not a forecast and not an account of why the won moved as it did. The weekly rate changes use one data source's Friday quote, and only the 20 November close was checked against Seoul market reports. Nothing here is a reason to buy or sell anything, in won or in dollars. It is a statistic about two series, not about any one investor.
