Our 23 October piece reported that Japan's highest-yielding government bond was the 25-year, not the 40-year. That was true on 30 of the first 33 business days of our archive. It has stopped being true. On each of the nine business days from **9 to 19 November** the 40-year yield was the highest point on the curve.

## The count, 53 days on

We save the Ministry of Finance's yield curve every day, because the Ministry's file holds only the current month and drops older days at each month's turn. We now hold **53 business days**, 1 September to 19 November.

| Highest-yielding maturity | Days |
|---|---|
| 25-year | 40 |
| 40-year | 13 |
| Any other | 0 |

The 40-year led on 1 September, 5 and 6 October, 28 October, and then every day from 9 to 19 November. In September it led on 1 of 19 days, in October on 3 of 21 and in November on 9 of 13.

## How far ahead

On 19 November the long end looked like this:

| Maturity | Yield, 19 Nov 2026 |
|---|---|
| 20 years | 3.745% |
| 25 years | 4.038% |
| 30 years | 4.030% |
| 40 years | **4.092%** |

The 40-year yielded 5.4 basis points more than the 25-year and 6.2 more than the 30-year. A 5.4-point lead is the widest the 40-year has had over the 25-year in our 53 days; the 25-year's widest lead over the 40-year was 4.0 points. On 6 and 9 November the two were within 0.2 points of each other.

## What happened between 6 and 19 November

On 6 November the 10-year yield fell 11.0 basis points in one day, the largest one-day fall of the 52 day-to-day changes we can compute, to 2.696%. It was also the low of the period for the 10-, 20- and 25-year yields. Since then every maturity has risen:

| Maturity | 6 Nov | 19 Nov | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 year | 1.558% | 1.659% | +10.1bp |
| 2 years | 1.732% | 1.853% | +12.1bp |
| 5 years | 2.070% | 2.235% | +16.5bp |
| 10 years | 2.696% | 2.870% | +17.4bp |
| 20 years | 3.599% | 3.745% | +14.6bp |
| 25 years | 3.926% | 4.038% | +11.2bp |
| 30 years | 3.898% | 4.030% | +13.2bp |
| 40 years | 3.925% | 4.092% | +16.7bp |

The 40-year gained 16.7 points, more than the 25-year's 11.2, which is why it passed it. The 10-year gained the most, 17.4, and the 1-year the least, 10.1. The 40-year's low in our archive was 3.900% on 26 October, so from there to 19 November it is up 19.2 basis points.

## The whole period

Since 1 September the short end is still higher and the long end lower, as in our earlier piece:

| Maturity | 1 Sep | 19 Nov | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 year | 1.527% | 1.659% | +13.2bp |
| 2 years | 1.802% | 1.853% | +5.1bp |
| 5 years | 2.280% | 2.235% | -4.5bp |
| 10 years | 2.987% | 2.870% | -11.7bp |
| 20 years | 3.859% | 3.745% | -11.4bp |
| 25 years | 4.143% | 4.038% | -10.5bp |
| 30 years | 4.131% | 4.030% | -10.1bp |
| 40 years | 4.145% | 4.092% | -5.3bp |

The 40-year yield was 4.145% on 1 September and 4.092% on 19 November, almost the same. The 30-year minus 10-year spread was 114.4 basis points on 1 September, 120.2 at its widest on 6 November, and 116.0 on 19 November. At its narrowest, on 1 October, it was 103.0.

## What this does not show

We do not know why the 40-year has overtaken the 25-year; the difference of a few basis points is small, and the 40-year had led on four earlier days (1 September, 5 and 6 October and 28 October) before this run. The archive is 53 days long and ends on 19 November. Nothing here is a forecast of Japanese yields, and nothing is advice on bonds.
