Retained earnings are the profits a company has kept since it began, minus the losses. When the figure is negative, the company has lost more than it has earned over its life, on the books. We read it from each company's fiscal-2025 annual report.

## The count

**598 of 2,463** Korean listed companies had negative retained earnings: **24.3%**.

| Market | Companies | With deficit | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| KOSPI | 785 | 95 | 12.1% |
| KOSDAQ | 1,668 | 497 | 29.8% |
| All | 2,463 | 598 | 24.3% |

## But small in value

Together the 598 were worth **119.9 trillion won** on 12 November 2026, or **2.7%** of the 4,484.7 trillion won total. Many companies, little value.

## Not all losing, not all cheap

**488** of the 598 reported a net loss or zero for 2025, but **110** reported a profit. The deficit companies' median price-to-book was **1.17**, higher than the **0.63** median of companies with non-negative retained earnings.

So the market, on the day measured, did not price accumulated losses as a reason for a lower book multiple. The table is a count, and it does not say why.

*This is statistics, not you or your company.*
