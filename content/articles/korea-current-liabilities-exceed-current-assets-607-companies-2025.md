Current assets are cash and things that turn into cash within a year. Current liabilities are the bills due within a year. When the first is smaller than the second, the balance sheet shows more due than on hand. We read both from each company's fiscal-2025 annual report.

## The count

**607 of 2,499** companies had current assets below current liabilities: **24.3%**.

| Market | Companies | Below 1.0 | Share | Median ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| KOSPI | 770 | 239 | 31.0% | 1.34 |
| KOSDAQ | 1,716 | 358 | 20.9% | 1.92 |
| All | 2,499 | 607 | 24.3% | not computed |

The ratio is current assets divided by current liabilities. For **137** of the 607, it was below 0.5.

## Large companies are in it

The five largest by market value among the 607 were:

| Company | Market cap (12 Nov 2026) | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Samsung SDI | 38.1 trillion won | 0.89 |
| HD Hyundai Heavy Industries | 28.1 trillion | 0.98 |
| Korea Electric Power | 19.7 trillion | 0.46 |
| Samsung Heavy Industries | 14.6 trillion | 0.79 |
| Korean Air | 11.7 trillion | 0.64 |

Together the 607 were **8.1%** of market value: a quarter of the companies, a twelfth of the value.

## Profitable, and still short

**251** of the 607 reported a positive net income for 2025. A shortfall on the balance sheet and a profit on the income statement went together for about four in ten.

## By industry

Retail Trade had **21 of 41** companies below 1.0 (51.2%), and Food Products Manufacturing **26 of 73** (35.6%). Medical, Precision, and Optical Instruments had **8 of 100** (8.0%). These are counts within industries of at least 30 companies.

## What this does not say

A year-end balance sheet is a photograph. We have no cash flow or loan maturities, and some businesses are paid before they pay. We do not say any company is at risk.

*This is statistics, not you or your company.*
