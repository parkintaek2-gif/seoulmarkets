Price-to-book is market capitalisation divided by the equity a company reported at its last year-end. Below 1.0, the market values the company at less than its accounting net worth.

On 12 November 2026, **1,588 of the 2,542 Korean listed companies** that have a positive-equity ratio traded below 1.0. That is **62.5%**.

## Count is not value

| Market | Companies with PBR | Below 1.0 | Share of count | Share of market cap | Median PBR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KOSPI | 810 | 606 | 74.8% | 22.7% | 0.49 |
| KOSDAQ | 1,723 | 979 | 56.8% | 21.0% | 0.85 |
| All (incl. KONEX) | 2,542 | 1,588 | 62.5% | 22.6% | 0.73 |

The two shares do not match because the big companies are not the cheap ones. Below-book companies are 62.5% of the list but 22.6% of its value. Among the 50 largest by market cap, 21 are below 1.0.

## What sits under 1.0

The 1,588 are not all loss-makers. **1,048** reported a positive return on equity for 2025 and **540** reported zero or negative. At the other end, 306 companies (12.0% of the 2,542) trade at 3.0 or above.

One reading of a median is that half the companies are below it. On KOSPI, half trade below 0.49 times their book value.

*This is statistics, not you or your portfolio. It describes how 2,542 companies were priced on one day.*
