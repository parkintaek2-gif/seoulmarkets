The equity ratio is equity divided by total assets: how much of what a company holds is funded by its owners rather than lenders. Using the latest annual filing for each of 3,706 Tokyo-listed companies, the median is 56.9%. In our earlier count of 2,559 Korean listed companies the median was 60.3%.

The medians are about three and a half points apart. The distributions are different in a more interesting way.

## The Japanese spread

| Equity as a share of assets | Companies | Share of companies | Share of combined assets |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Below zero | 11 | 0.3% | 0.0% |
| 0 to under 20% | 245 | 6.6% | 73.6% |
| 20 to under 40% | 671 | 18.1% | 11.2% |
| 40 to under 60% | 1,125 | 30.4% | 8.9% |
| 60 to under 80% | 1,228 | 33.1% | 5.1% |
| 80% or more | 426 | 11.5% | 1.3% |

The company at the 10th percentile has 26% equity and the one at the 90th has 81%. The thinnest positive band is only 6.6% of companies, but it holds 73.6% of the combined yen total assets. With the 11 negatives, 256 companies (6.9%) are under 20%. That is because it contains the banks. All 83 Japanese banks in the tape are at 18.5% or below, with a median of 5.0%. Securities firms have a median of 41.9%, insurers 27.1% and other financing companies 32.5%.

## Leaving finance out

Remove those four financial groups (172 companies) and 3,535 non-financial companies are left. Their median is 57.8%, 144 of them are below 20%, and 1,634 (46.2%) are at 60% or more. Among industries with at least 40 companies, pharmaceutical firms were highest at a median 69.7% and real estate lowest at 34.4%, with machinery at 66.8%, information and communication at 64.4%, retail at 47.3% and land transportation at 41.9%.

## Against Korea

| | Japan (3,706) | Korea (2,559) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Median | 56.9% | 60.3% |
| 20% or below, including negatives | 256 (6.9%) | 128 (5.0%) |
| 60% or above | 1,654 (44.6%) | 1,294 (50.6%) |

In both countries close to half of listed companies are at 60% or above. Korea has slightly more at the high end. Japan has slightly more at the low end, and the 11 Japanese companies with negative equity, including Japan Display, are a small but visible group.

## What cannot be compared

Of the 3,706 Japanese rows, 2,928 are consolidated and 778 are parent-only, and a parent-only company's ratio is not comparable with a group's. We did not adjust. Most Japanese rows are for the year ended 31 March 2026, while the Korean figures are fiscal 2025, so the dates are not aligned. Two Japanese companies have no usable ratio and are left out, not set to zero.

A low ratio is not a verdict. A bank lives on deposits, and its 5% is how banks are built.
